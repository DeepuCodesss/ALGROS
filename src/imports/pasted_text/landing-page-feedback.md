1. Change the headline — this is the biggest issue

Current:

See Your Business Online Before You Pay.

Good concept, but it's a little generic and focuses on payment rather than the exciting outcome.

I'd make it:

See What Your Business Could Look Like Online.

Then the highlighted line:

Before You Invest in the Full Website.

Or, more sales-focused:

Your New Website. Designed Before You Commit.

That second one is particularly strong because it immediately explains the mechanism.

Your first 10–20 seconds are critical, and the headline needs to communicate the value proposition immediately.

2. Don't call it a “sample”

This is a surprisingly important positioning change.

Sample website sounds cheap/free/throwaway.

You're actually offering:

A custom website preview built specifically for their business.

So I'd consistently use:

CUSTOM WEBSITE PREVIEW

and:

Get your homepage designed around your actual business, brand, photos and goals — before committing to the full website.

That makes ₹1,499 feel much more justified.

3. Make the ₹1,499 card sell the outcome, not just the price

Right now the eye goes:

₹1,499 → payment → 48 hours

I'd restructure it visually as:

CUSTOM WEBSITE PREVIEW

₹1,499

A complete custom homepage for your business

✓ Built around your actual brand
✓ Your logo, photos & content
✓ Desktop + mobile design
✓ Delivered within 48 hours
✓ ₹1,499 credited toward your full website

Then the CTA:

GET MY CUSTOM PREVIEW →

That's much stronger than:

GET MY PREVIEW FOR ₹1,499 →

The latter sounds like you're buying a file. The first sounds like you're unlocking something.

Clear, action-oriented CTA language and a focused above-the-fold structure are consistently recommended for landing pages.

4. Make the “credited toward full website” benefit HUGE

Honestly, this may be your strongest sales mechanism.

Right now:

₹1,499 fully credited if you proceed

is buried in the bullet list.

I'd make it a visual risk-reversal badge directly underneath the price:

₹1,499 FULLY CREDITED
toward your website when you proceed.

So psychologically the customer isn't thinking:

“I'm spending ₹1,499 on a sample.”

They're thinking:

“I'm basically putting ₹1,499 toward the actual website and getting to see it first.”

That is a MUCH better proposition.

Removing uncertainty and addressing objections near the CTA can materially improve conversion; HubSpot specifically highlights these “doubt removers” around CTAs.

5. Add a visual example of what they actually receive

This is the biggest missing piece visually.

Your page says:

“full custom homepage”

But I don't actually see the homepage.

That's a problem.

Put a gorgeous browser-frame mockup directly beside/behind the offer showing:

“Here's what your custom preview actually looks like.”

Even better:

Before → Custom Preview

For example:

YOUR CURRENT WEBSITE
     ↓
     [ ugly / outdated screenshot ]

       48 HOURS

YOUR ALGROS PREVIEW
     ↓
     [ beautiful redesigned website ]

You're selling a visual product. Let them see the transformation.

Strong imagery that reinforces the offer is a standard landing-page foundation, and proof should appear where users are deciding whether the offer is credible.

6. Add proof immediately under the CTA

This is probably the biggest trust hole in the current design.

Under:

GET MY CUSTOM PREVIEW →

put something like:

★★★★★
“Our new website finally looks like the business we built.”
— Business Owner, [Industry]

Or, even better:

real client result + screenshot + name/company, assuming you have permission to use it.

Social proof close to CTAs can help reduce hesitation; HubSpot cites experiments where social proof placed under CTAs improved conversion, while also warning that weak or irrelevant proof can backfire.

So don't fabricate testimonials. One real, specific testimonial is worth much more than five generic ones.

7. Your current copy has too much “feature language”

You have:

Fully designed homepage
Your real photos, logo & content used
Mobile-first, premium design
Delivered in 48 hours

These are good, but you're mostly describing what you do.

Shift toward what they get.

For example:

Know exactly what your business could look like online.

Make a confident decision before investing in the full website.

Walk away with a premium homepage designed around your business.

The customer isn't buying “a homepage.”

They're buying confidence before a larger purchase.

That's your real product.

8. Add an “Only ₹1,499 to see it first” moment

You can create a beautiful price comparison:

BUILD BLIND

₹20,000–₹50,000+

Commit to a website without seeing the finished direction.

SEE IT FIRST

₹1,499

Get your custom homepage designed first.

₹1,499 → credited toward the full project.

That gives your offer a powerful anchor.

Just make sure the full-project price range reflects your actual pricing. Don't create an artificial inflated comparison.

9. Add a tiny FAQ beneath the hero

People buying from an unfamiliar agency will have questions.

Answer the obvious ones:

What exactly do I receive?
A custom-designed homepage based on your business, branding, content and imagery.

Can I request changes?
Give your actual revision policy here.

What happens after 48 hours?
The customer receives the preview and can decide whether to continue.

What happens to my ₹1,499?
It's fully credited toward the full website project if you proceed.

This is especially important because uncertainty around payment and what happens next creates friction. Research-backed checkout guidance strongly emphasizes making costs, next steps, and expectations explicit.

And one thing I'd absolutely NOT do

Don't add 15 trust badges, fake urgency, fake countdown timers, “ONLY 3 SLOTS LEFT!!!”, fake client logos, or a giant “100% GUARANTEED” banner.

For a premium agency, that can make the page feel more like a template selling course than a serious studio.

Your current dark navy + cream + gold aesthetic is actually pretty strong. I'd keep that and make the content hierarchy more persuasive, rather than throwing more decoration at it.
